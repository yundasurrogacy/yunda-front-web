What Should You Prepare Before the Conference?

You do not need every decision finalized before speaking with providers. A short status sheet, however, makes each conversation more useful.

  • Your family-building goals and whether one or both intended fathers are considering providing sperm.
  • Whether you have selected an egg donor, created embryos, or chosen an IVF clinic.
  • Your residence, preferred treatment location, travel constraints, and expected communication needs.
  • A working budget range that separates agency services from medical, donor, legal, insurance, escrow, travel, and contingency costs.
  • The decisions for which you still need independent medical, legal, insurance, or financial guidance.

Questions About Agency Scope and Case Ownership

  • Which services does your agency provide directly, and which are handled by independent third parties?
  • Who owns our case day to day, and how often should we expect updates?
  • How do you coordinate intended parents, the gestational carrier, the IVF clinic, attorneys, insurance specialists, and escrow?
  • What support is available before matching, during pregnancy, around delivery, and after birth?
  • How do you support gay and international intended parents whose journeys involve additional travel, language, or document handoffs?
  • What happens when a match, medical plan, insurance assumption, or timeline changes?

Questions About Screening and Matching

Ask each provider to distinguish its own screening from the independent medical, psychological, and legal reviews performed by qualified professionals. The American Society for Reproductive Medicine discusses informed consent, independent legal advice, health care, and counseling in gestational-carrier arrangements.

  • What information is collected before a potential gestational carrier is introduced to intended parents?
  • Which evaluations occur before matching, and which occur later through the clinic or other professionals?
  • How are expectations about communication, medical decisions, travel, termination, delivery, and post-birth contact discussed?
  • How much choice do both sides have, and what happens if either side decides not to proceed?
  • How are re-matching responsibilities and costs explained?

Screening and matching can organize risk review and expectations; they do not guarantee a match, pregnancy, or birth outcome.

Questions About Egg Donation and IVF Coordination

Many gay intended parents will need an egg donor and an IVF clinic, but the exact medical plan is individual. The CDC ART glossary provides neutral terminology, while a qualified fertility clinic should explain options and recommendations for a specific case.

  • Who helps us understand egg-donor options, screening records, and the handoff to the IVF clinic?
  • Which decisions belong to the clinic rather than the agency?
  • How are donor, clinic, laboratory, transport, and legal timelines coordinated?
  • How will we receive updates when several professionals are involved?
  • Which costs are included in an agency estimate and which are billed separately?

Questions About Legal Coordination and Parentage

Surrogacy law and procedure depend on the relevant jurisdiction and individual facts. An agency can coordinate introductions and milestones, but independent attorneys should explain agreements, rights, responsibilities, and parentage steps.

  • When should each party retain separate counsel?
  • Which jurisdiction may govern the agreement and parentage process?
  • Who tracks legal clearance before medical treatment begins?
  • How are parentage documents, hospital coordination, and post-birth steps managed?
  • What additional planning may apply to international intended parents?

Frequently Asked Questions

How does surrogacy for gay men generally work?

A journey may involve sperm from one or both intended fathers, an egg donor, IVF and embryo creation, a gestational carrier, medical and psychological screening, separate legal representation, insurance and escrow planning, pregnancy coordination, and a parentage process. The sequence and requirements depend on the people involved and the relevant jurisdictions.

Do gay intended parents always need an egg donor and a gestational carrier?

Gay male intended parents pursuing gestational surrogacy generally need eggs to create embryos and a gestational carrier to carry a pregnancy. The specific donor, embryo, and treatment plan should be discussed with a qualified fertility clinic, while legal arrangements should be reviewed by independent counsel.

What should we ask a surrogacy agency at MHB New York 2026?

Ask how responsibilities are divided across screening, matching, egg donation, IVF coordination, legal work, insurance review, escrow, communication, delivery, and post-birth support. Request written explanations of included and excluded costs, identify the professionals who make each decision, and compare every provider using the same questions.

Should we choose the agency with the lowest estimate?

Not without checking whether the estimates cover the same services and assumptions. Compare agency fees, third-party expenses, variable medical and insurance items, legal and escrow charges, re-match terms, refund rules, and contingencies. A lower headline total may simply exclude categories that appear elsewhere later.

When should intended parents speak with an attorney?

Legal input should begin early enough to inform location, agreement, and parentage planning before binding documents or medical milestones. Each party should receive advice appropriate to their circumstances from qualified independent counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.
